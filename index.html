Essay

The Atrophy of Judgment

AI may be changing the faculty by which human beings are meant to govern its use: judgment.

Megi Pishtari

That faculty is easiest to appreciate when it fails. A colleague forwards an AI-generated executive summary to a room full of decision-makers without opening the document it claims to represent. The summary is clean, coherent, plausible. What it omits is a qualification buried deep in the original text, the one complication that should have changed the decision. Nobody notices. The summary is good enough.

Interactive  ·  Instrument 01

Do the same review twice, and see what a supplied estimate changed in you.

Three charity cases arrive with an estimate already supplied, and three with nothing filled in. There is no time limit. It runs entirely in your browser. While the model endpoint is off, as it is in this published build, no responses are sent or stored.

The argument

Agential drift is the incremental transfer of evaluative authority to AI through delegations that each look reasonable.

As AI systems become more capable, the human role often moves upstream: from execution to framing, monitoring, approval, and responsibility. That makes human judgment more important, not less. But it also makes judgment harder to evaluate. What drift costs is the judgment that oversight depends on.

A person may receive a better output while becoming less able to explain, challenge, or independently reconstruct the reasoning behind it. They may feel more confident while their evaluative range narrows. The Drift Meter begins from that tension. It treats oversight as a human capacity, not only a governance process.

Reading becomes receiving. Authoring becomes approving.

What it measures

Five measures that can carry a result

  1. 1Evidence engagement · primary outcome, fixed in advanceWhether each case was looked at. Opening the supplied read or the evidence panel marks it.
  2. 2Revision behaviourHow many of the supplied assumptions the reader moved. Scored only in the round where something was supplied to revise.
  3. 3Ambiguity toleranceUncertainty flagged, and calls to investigate rather than to fund or pass.
  4. 4Framing autonomyHow far the reader departed from the frame they were handed. Undefined where no frame was supplied.
  5. 5Confidence calibrationThe reader’s own confidence, printed beside what they did. The two are never subtracted.

And two that ask whether the answer was right

They are here so that a run can count against this project’s own hypothesis rather than only confirm it. Neither can carry a result yet.

Estimate accuracy stays undefined until a supported value is written for each assumption, and inventing those values to complete it would be the failure this project is about.

The planted-error catch rate rests on one planted error per slate, which for a single reader is one observation rather than a rate.

The supplied estimate is credited, at random, to an AI model, to a senior program officer, or to an unnamed prior reviewer. The number, the reasoning and the recommendation are identical in all three. Only the authority attached to them changes.

The future study

Six predictions, registered before anyone has taken part

They belong to a study that has not been run, and the Drift Meter is the instrument that study would use. Each was registered with the result that would falsify it, so none can be chosen after the fact.

  1. P1Evidence engagement is lower in the round where an estimate is supplied than in the round where none is.
  2. P2Fewer of the supplied assumptions are moved when the estimate is credited to an AI model than when it is credited to no one.Amended before any data existed, as amendments A1 and A2. The registered wording is printed in the protocol.
  3. P3Self-rated confidence is higher after the assisted round than after the control round, whatever the working shows.
  4. P4The planted error is caught less often when the case arrives with a recommendation already selected than when it does not.
  5. P6Readers who caught the planted error name the right figure on the unseen transfer case more often than readers who missed it.
  6. P5The drop in scrutiny is larger in the AI-attributed arm than in the human-attributed arm, and both are larger than the unattributed arm.

The record

Corrections, kept in view

This is a prototype, and it is built to claim only what it can observe. A project about unearned confidence keeps its own errors on the page. They are recorded, not deleted.
